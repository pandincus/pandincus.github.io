# What the heck is NWN?

_(And, why should this game from 2001 be my introduction to this blog?)_

## A Little History

I've been gaming for a good number of years -- since I was a child (on the Nintendo Entertainment System, mind you), and I'm forty now. But Bioware's [Neverwinter Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neverwinter_Nights) from 2001 was a fairly defining game for me, for a couple of key reasons.

First, it was probably the first [CRPG](https://geektogeekmedia.com/geekery/video-games/what-is-a-crpg/) that I truly sat down and engaged with. I'd played Baldur's Gate 1 earlier, though I was honestly a bit too young to play it _well_, and mostly I had engaged with JRPGs on consoles before that (Dragon Warrior 1 for the NES was similarly defining, as well as Final Fantasy 6, but that's a post for another day and time).

Second, it is a game that I've come back to multiple times in my life. In the early 2010s, when Alli was getting into gaming (another good post in the making, there, but the short version of that story is Flash Point and Click Games --> Civilization --> The Sims --> RPGs), co-op NWN was her "gateway drug" to the deeper world of roleplaying games. In the late 2010s, I revisited it and got somewhat ~~addicted~~ obsessed with the world of NWN RP, specifically the server [Arelith](https://nwnarelith.com/). (_More on that another time, too, but a couple thousand hours later, I had a lovely, start-and-close story of a character and they managed to make a lasting impression in a persistent world. Wild times._)

## Neverwinter Nights, In a Nutshell

But I haven't really explained what it is, and why. I'll keep it short. Neverwinter Nights is a rather impressive implementation of [Dungeons and Dragons 3.5e](http://dnd3-5.wikidot.com/), made in 2001. I've always been fairly excited by D&D 3.5e. It is wonderfully "crunchy" in a way that 5th edition isn't. Character building is incredibly complicated (more on that later), and there's something deliciously rewarding about getting to see a series of build choices play out impactfully. Not that 5th edition doesn't have this, it just doesn't quite have as much "bite" behind it.

NWN shipped with an original campaign spanning four meaty acts, though the campaign itself is not considered an excellent story, and is quite tedious in parts. The two major expansions, Shadows of Undretide and Hordes of the Underdark, are very highly regarded and you can play them without even having played the OC. But most importantly, and by miles, it shipped with an extensive [toolset](https://nwn.fandom.com/wiki/Toolset) that allowed the player community to make entire campaigns of their own -- full-fledged stories with NPCs, quests, characters, environments to explore, dungeons, and included a full scripting language ([NWScript](https://nwn.fandom.com/wiki/NWScript)) for controlling the module.

_[And boy, did the player community create!](https://neverwintervault.org/article/reference/campaigns-and-module-series-list-nwn1)_

I've played several of these back in the early 2010s, but I honestly cannot remember which I finished and which I didn't. Alli and I would often look for ones designed for co-op play, which, perhaps surprisingly, seemed to us at the time to be a very few number of them.

But the time has come! I want to indulge in some D&D 3.5e content, some crunchy build mechanics, and some engaging stories.

## Swordflight

[Swordflight](https://neverwintervault.org/project/nwn1/module/swordflight-chapter-one) is a NWVault Hall of Fame campaign spanning (currently) five chapters, created by [rogueknight333](https://www.patreon.com/c/u84646917/about). Many veteran NWN players consider it to be one of the [best modules of all time](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2018/11/Swordflight-Chapter-Four-has-been-released.html), which is high praise!

It is unfortunately single-player only, so Alli and I didn't play it back in our NWN hey-day. I gave it a whirl on my own back in 2022, but real life was busy at the time, so I didn't stick it out.

I've a little more free time on my plate now, so it seems a fitting module to pick to revisit NWN and get back into the fold.

That being said, Swordflight is also considered __difficult__. The [readme](https://neverwintervault.org/sites/neverwintervault.org/files/project/663/files/swordflight_chapter_one_readme_v4.txt) for the first chapter says plainly:

> 4) Recommended Difficulty Setting: This game was balanced for and mostly tested on the D&D Hardcore difficulty setting, though one can play on an easier setting if desired. It is intended to be quite challenging even on the D&D Hardcore setting, so playing at the higher difficulty setting is definitely not recommended.

_I've heard that future chapters are significantly harder than the first, as well (I got up to the middle of Chapter 2, last go-around)_

Despite all of my experience with NWN, I am a _terrible_ builder. But that's okay -- we've got tools and guidance out there to help us!

# Building an NWN Character

To be clear, if you're reading this and you're new to NWN, you can just go ahead and get started playing the original campaign. It isn't that difficult, and while you can definitely make terrible character-building choices, you should be able to get through it.

But ofter the OC, you'll probably want to sink your teeth into what makes a good character. __You will not find build guidance on my blog.__ I'm bad at it, and there are incredible NWN-build experts out there on the internet ([lilura1](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2021/01/Neverwinter-Nights-NWN-Best-Build-Class-Solo-Beginner.html), [tapatalk](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2021/01/Neverwinter-Nights-NWN-Best-Build-Class-Solo-Beginner.html), [steam](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2161046705) and more)

And the most important tool of all (in my opinion, anyway): kamiryn's [CharacterBuildCalculator (CBC)](https://neverwintervault.org/project/nwn1/other/tool/characterbuildcalculator-cbc) which is a mammoth Excel Spreadsheet that lets you completely design a build, level 1-40, and the damn thing has _all of the NWN rules built-in_ to tell you what the resulting stats will be (and if you've made a mistake).

Since I'm using this as a journal-of-sorts, I will briefly take you through my character journey that I'll be using for Swordflight.

## Build Theme: Melee Cleric

I love [clerics](https://nwn.fandom.com/wiki/Cleric) in NWN. They are a versatile class, with access to divine magic without the need to hunt down scrolls to copy into their spellbook, and at the same time they get full armor and shield proficiency (and because their magic is divine, they can cast it in armor without any penalty, unlike those pesky arcane casters). Because of their versatility, you can play them different ways -- divine caster damage-dealers, party buffers, etc. In my case, I would like to take advantage of the buffing/supportive magic of the cleric class in order to stay alive and be protected against a diverse set of situations (because truly, I don't know what madness this module is going to throw at me), and at the same time, _I want to hit things really hard with a weapon_.

So the theme I was looking for was a melee-oriented cleric: high strength, lots of armor, and packing a punch. On top of that, I wanted an array of skill points available to not have the character be too much of a one-trick pony.

While experimenting with a build that combined Fighter, Cleric, and Champion of Torm, I came across [this build by Stagmeister](https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/nwnecbguild/viewtopic.php?p=30496), and inspiration struck: Rogue!

## The Build

I struggled for a while with this one. I kept falling into the trap of wanting everything -- Champion of Torm benefits from charisma, but my melee cleric already needed lots of Strength (for hitting), Wisdom (for spell-casting), and Constitution (for stayin' alive). Oh, and I needed some Intelligence (for skill points)! So in the end, I had to sacrifice the Charisma bonuses.

{{< figure src="cbc-berin.png" alt="My build in kamiryn's CBC" caption="Does this look like a game, to you? This is where the fun starts!" >}}

In the end, I settled on a Human Rogue (4) / Cleric (26) with Travel and Plant domains, Champion of Torm (10). Rogue gets you a few fun feats, sneak attack up to 2d6, and a _bunch_ of skill points. Cleric is cleric, and with the Plant domain I get Barkskin (more armor class), and with Travel the 5th-level spell of Haste. Champion of Torm for the full ten levels nets +1 BAB per level, good saves, bonus feats every other level, a d10 on hit dice, and +1 to all saving throws for every two levels.

## The Character: Berin Hull

_Who is this guy, anyway?_

This doesn't strictly matter for the game, but since NWN is a roleplaying game, and we will at times be presented with choices about how to go about resolving a situation, I wanted to understand who my character was.

I invented a lad named __Berin Hull__ in his mid-twenties, and he's on his way to becoming a full-fledged adventurer. (I chose to begin the campaign at level 3)

What I wrote for his backstory:

> For a young man in his twenties, there is an oddly stern and serious look to this one.

> Berin grew up in Athkatla, in the nation of Amn. Having lost his parents at a young age to war, he initially found himself in the company of youth gangs. Mostly, they engaged in mischief, though he did commit some light robbery (mostly of shops) in order to get by. He still wears the tattoos of one of the more notorious gangs of the region upon his wrists. On one more ambitious journey at the age of 14, he was forced to join a robbery of a caravan. The robbery went poorly -- the other, older rogues he was with were killed. The would-be-victim was a Paladin of Torm, who took pity on the young Berin, and brought him to his Church, believing a better upbringing would set him on the right path.

> He was mostly correct. Berin studied under the church for several years and, given guidance, found a liking to being a cleric. But he hasn't forgiven himself for sins of the past, and even blames himself for the deaths of his fellows. His mentors at the Church of Torm advised he seek his own path for a time as a carvan guard, to undo his past mistakes and spend time righting some wrongs in the world instead of causing them.

> Berin is now twenty-five. What adventure awaits him upon the road?

{{< figure src="nwn-1.png" alt="Character info screen showing Berin Hull's biography alongside the voice set list" caption="Athkatla, a youth gang, a robbery that went badly, and a Paladin of Torm who took pity, setting our hero on the path to divine worship" >}}

This isn't so unique of a backstory, but I wanted to understand his vibe: Berin is trying to redeem some past sins, which is a weight he carries with him. He's spent over a decade in the Church of Torm, so that has shaped his demeanor and his skillset. He's got a bit of wanderlust, and is seeking out ways to do good. And he is roughly familiar with the southwestern portion of Faerun, from Athkatla to Calimshan. (This is me reading ahead a bit to know that Swordflight takes place in Calimshan -- hey, any good DM will give you a bit of a story primer to inform your background!)

# Setting up the Character

Before jumping right into Swordflight, I built out the character up to level 3 in the [Pretty Good Character Creator/Customizer](https://neverwintervault.org/project/nwn1/module/pretty-good-character-creator-customizer-pgc3-powered-igipe). This is a tremendously useful tool because NWN doesn't give you (unlike, for example, Solasta) a built-in tool to create and level up character "outside" of an adventure. In my case, I am choosing to begin Swordflight with Berin Hull at level three, with some prior adventuring experience, giving him 1 Rogue level and 2 Cleric levels out of the gate. I need to level him up, and then equip him a little. (I opted for a set of half plate armor, a morningstar, a tower shield, and some healing potions).

{{< figure src="pgcc-berin.jpg" alt="Berin Hull in PGCC" caption="All geared up and ready to go!" >}}

Note that you can of course use tools like this to abuse a module. Swordflight lists starting from levels 1-3 as acceptable and supported in its [readme](https://neverwintervault.org/sites/neverwintervault.org/files/project/663/files/swordflight_chapter_one_readme_v4.txt):

> 2) Recommended Starting Level: One to Three. This module is primarily intended for first level characters with only the standard starting gear. However, such a character can expect to find that the game is quite challenging and requires a very tactical approach to combat. If you find such a level of challenge excessive, you may wish to try starting with a character of up to 3rd level, perhaps with somewhat more gold and gear as well, which will make the game a little less frustrating, especially in the early stages, while still remaining reasonably challenging. Note that starting with even a 3rd-level character you should not play carelessly: some encounters will scale, some foes are tough enough that even at a higher level they can take you on with some chance of success, and with experience scaling the farther you get into the game the less of an edge you will have over a player who started at Level One. Playing with an even higher level character is not impossible, but in my opinion risks degrading the challenge of the game to the point of tedium (also a few conversations in the game assume the character is low-level and lacking in magical weapons: obviously these will make less sense if that is not the case). A character starting at level 1 to 3 can expect to be at approximately level 5 (give or take a level) by completion of the module.

To make sure I didn't over-do it, the PGCC has a little neat-o tool that tells you if your character is about right for their level, or if they're overpowered (e.g. did I use PGCC to give myself a bunch of fancy gear).

{{< figure src="mirror-mirror.png" alt="Berin Hull in PGCC evaluating their wealth" caption="The mirror, mirror tool says that Berin is the fairest of them all!" >}}

I wanted to give Berin a set of full plate, but that eeked him out over the top of the wealth range, and I figured it made sense for someone of his age and status to NOT have a set, yet. The Church of Torm has taken care of him, but resources are limited, and full plate armor is significantly more expensive than half plate. Besides, this gives him an early personal mission of his own -- get better armor. Eventually, he'll be in that full plate! As it stands, he's doing just fine as-is with an AC of 20 and 1500 gold in his pocket. Sure, he could splurge right now on Full Plate, but that'd leave him nothing for the costs of the road: rations, consumables, a stay at an inn, who knows what else. Better to wait a bit, first.
